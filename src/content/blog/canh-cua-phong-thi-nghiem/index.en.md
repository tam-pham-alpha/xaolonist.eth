In the past, bringing a factory home meant taking machines apart, hiring trucks, and passing through many gates. In the world of AI, the fruit of a factory worth billions of dollars can sometimes sit inside data files. If those files are copied, the thief does not need to rebuild everything from the start

Those important files are usually called model weights. You can picture them as memory crystallized after learning. Source code tells how to build a brain. Weights hold what that brain learned after consuming an enormous amount of machines and electricity. Keeping the code while losing the weights can be like still having the blueprint of a school after every student has been taken somewhere else

Leopold argues that this is one of the most dangerous weaknesses of AI labs. Many companies grew up in a startup culture where speed, sharing, and experimentation come before tightness. That way of working is effective when what you need to protect is a commercial product. It becomes fragile if the asset inside can create an economic or military advantage for an entire country

The threat then changes shape as well. An ordinary attacker wants money, customer data, or a chance to extort. A nation can be more patient, spend years planting people, look for the weakest link in the supply chain, or wait for one careless employee. The main door may be very strong, while the real key sits on the computer of a small contractor almost no one notices

Security is therefore not only building more firewalls. It requires redesigning hardware, splitting access, controlling who can touch important assets, and preparing for the case that an opponent is already inside. The tighter you close the door, the slower research becomes and the harder an open culture is to keep. The more you open it, the greater the risk that years of investment are copied in a single night

There is a paradox here. Science moves forward because people publish, verify, and build on one another's discoveries. National security exists because some knowledge does not leave the room. If AI grows stronger, society will have to decide which part should be a common asset of humanity and which part needs to be kept closed, at least for a time

He does not know how wide the laboratory door should open. He only knows that what stands behind the door is changing faster than the locks that protect it. At some point the question is no longer who can build the strongest intelligence, but who can keep it long enough to decide what to do next
