For a long time AI was told as a story of companies. The company with the better model would sell more products, attract more users, and make more money. The competition therefore looked like earlier technology races, where the winner gained market share and users gained more choices

Leopold sees a different boundary. If AI only helps write emails and make images, it is still a product. If it can speed up scientific research, design weapons, find network holes, or operate millions of machines, it becomes a source of power. Then the gap between two models no longer appears only on a leaderboard. It can enter the economic and military capacity of the country that owns them

History has many times turned around a technology like that. Coal fed the factories, oil carried armies farther, and semiconductors became the bloodstream of the modern economy. AI is different because it does not only supply energy to the hand. It can support the processes of thinking, inventing, and organizing, the very things that help a country turn every other resource into strength

So the race will not happen only among laboratories. It will pull in access to chips, electricity, data, talent, and alliances that control supply chains. A decision that looks commercial, such as which chips to sell to whom, can also be a decision about security. A data center that looks like a company asset can be seen as strategic infrastructure

This way of seeing easily leads people into a dangerous spiral. When each side believes that second place loses everything, they have reason to run faster, keep more secrets, and accept more risk. Safety becomes something that can wait, cooperation becomes a sign of weakness, and every defensive step by one side looks like preparation for attack in the eyes of the other

Leopold places a clear bet on democracies keeping an advantage over authoritarian governments. The moral ground of that argument is easy to understand, but a technological advantage does not by itself make a country wise. Great power still needs law, oversight, and the ability to restrain itself. If not, the one who holds the flame in the name of something good can still burn down the house

He thinks the hard question is not which country should win. The harder question is how a race can have a winner without humanity losing together. When intelligence becomes power, we need an order strong enough to stop a bad actor from seizing it, and clear-eyed enough not to turn that fear into a reason to give every power to a single hand
