He has come to feel more and more that work is not only about making money. Money is simply the part that is easiest to see. What quietly accumulates behind it is a sharper mind, a life with rhythm and order, and something he still likes to call good fortune

A person who stays idle for too long can slowly grow dull. Not because they lack knowledge, but because they lack the friction that forces them to think, decide, take responsibility, make mistakes and correct them. Good work is like a whetstone. A little sharpening each day clears some of the fog from the mind, improves judgment, and makes a person less likely to be carried away by emotions or trivial things

He thinks good fortune may come partly from there. As long as a person still creates value, helps someone, keeps a promise, and finishes what they have taken responsibility for, they are maintaining a current between themselves and the world. Money, opportunities, good relationships, or the trust of other people may simply be different forms in which that current finds its way back

That is why he still wants to work, even if one day he no longer needs money very much. He works so his mind does not gather dust, so he can remain useful, so each morning there is still something worthy of his attention. He does not need to do a great deal. It is enough to keep creating a little value, to keep moving, and to remain connected with life

Work, however, is also like practicing with a sword. Train enough and the blade becomes sharp. Train until exhaustion and both the sword and the person begin to chip. Perhaps good fortune has less to do with working more or working less, and more to do with staying clear enough to know what is worth doing, then doing it well
